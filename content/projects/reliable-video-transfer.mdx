A client/server pair in C, <Claim id="udp-loc">416 lines total</Claim>, that transfers video files over
raw UDP sockets and layers its own reliability mechanism on top —
essentially a hand-rolled subset of what TCP gives you for free.

Packets are sequence-numbered so the receiver can detect gaps and reorder
arrivals; the server tracks per-packet delivery state and drives
retransmission requests for anything missing. That asymmetry — the server
does most of the ACK bookkeeping — matches a design where the receiver, not
the sender, decides what's still outstanding.

This is a learning exercise, not a production transfer tool, and I'd frame
it that way in an interview rather than defending it as a real alternative
to TCP: the honest answer to "why not just use TCP" is that the point was
implementing the reliability layer myself, not shipping the fastest
possible transfer.
